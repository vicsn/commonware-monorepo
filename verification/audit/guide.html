Auditing the Merkle proofs

Thirteen steps for checking that the Hax → DyLean proofs about Merkle trees say what is claimed of the Rust. Part I is the inclusion of one item. Part II is an ordered read under a checkpoint root. Wherever one piece of code must agree with another, both are shown side by side with what links them.

The proofs live on branch verify/qmdb-hax-dylean, in the checkout that holds this page. qmdb/README.md states the results of part I and qmdb/mmb/README.md those of part II; this guide is for the person who checks them. It is written for a reader who knows Rust and Merkle trees and has not used Lean or DyLean. No step asks you to read a proof.

Production baseline: b45ef2d78ea7535d8a51588912ba1e13e461b146. The panels and tables marked as generated are built from the sources by verification/audit/build.py, and check.sh fails when they are stale.

Out of scope: the hax pipeline. This audit assumes that hax, Charon and Aeneas translate the Rust faithfully into Lean, and that their models of the Rust core library match Rust. It does not audit them. Step 4 only checks how the pipeline is used: which tools are pinned, that regenerating the Lean reproduces the committed files, and that nothing was filled in by hand. A translation bug that changes the meaning of the verifier would leave every theorem here true of the wrong program.

Scope and assumptions

In scope: the Merkle target of the Hax → DyLean experiment, in two parts. For part I that is the production files it shares with the proof, the verification-only client, the handwritten Lean that states what the Rust means, the application assumption, the protocol model, the theorem statements and the checks that a machine runs. For part II it is the specification of an ordered read, the production files proved to refine it, what it assumes of the writer, and its checks. The BLS and SAKE targets are out of scope; they share steps 4 to 7. So is the proof that a consumer builds on part II: no statement here says what a checkpoint is or who authenticates it.

Four things are assumed and not audited. For each, the audit still checks how the proof depends on it.

A3DyLean’s core: traces, what the attacker knows, reachability and its tactics.Its pin, the patch applied to it, the three #combine blocks that define what statements mean, and the two actions accept and receive.6
A4The hash is ideal. A digest is DyLean’s hash constructor applied to its input, as ProVerif and Tamarin treat a hash by default: two digests are equal only when their inputs are the same term, and concatenation keeps the boundary between parts. SHA-256 allows length extension and the ideal hash does not, which is irrelevant here: extension derives the digest of a longer input from a digest alone, so it matters only when part of an input is secret, and every input the verifier hashes is public or chosen by the attacker. Part II adds a second constructor, for Keccak-256, to what a term is. No cited statement mentions it; a consumer hashes its keys with it.What the reading of the hash says, against what production hashes. What the ideal hash cannot express is written down. In part II, that a path through a root is a list of whole fields and not a byte offset.5, 8

Every Lean declaration, by trust type

The trust chains say which links hold. This graph says which Lean a person has to read. It holds every Lean declaration of both parts: the handwritten definitions and theorems, the generated definitions they reach, and the three libraries under both. Lean prints the graph, and the trust type of each declaration follows from it.

  • cited statement A theorem that this guide cites, in one of three groups: inclusion, ordered reads in the model, and the model against extracted Rust. Its statement is a reading that step 1 or step 8 checks. Lean checks its proof.
  • trusted Handwritten, and a cited statement depends on what it says. Steps 5, 6 and 9 read these.
  • generated Produced from Rust by hax from the extracted crate. Assumed faithful (A1); steps 4 and 11 look at some of them.
  • assumed library DyLean (A3), the Aeneas library and hax’s core models (A1).
  • checked A lemma, or a definition that only proofs use. Lean checks it, and it is not to be read.

The rule behind the colours: start from a cited statement and follow what its statement mentions, then what the bodies of those definitions mention, and so on. Never enter a proof. Whatever is reached is what the statement says. A mistake in a checked declaration can make the build fail. It cannot change what a cited statement says.

The statements of part I and the declarations they depend on follow a fixed layout of at most 80 columns, which the build of this page enforces. Those of part II are shown as their author wrote them, at up to 100 columns.

Part II · Ordered reads under a checkpoint root

Steps 8 to 12. A reader holds a root that it trusts and receives a claim about one key with a proof: the key has this value, or the key has none. Part II is about when such a claim can be believed. Its root is the canonical root of a current database over an MMB, in the format of Commonware v2026.11.0. Tempo pins that code through klkvr/monorepo 69cc03365, upstream v2026.11.0 plus one commit to partitioned ordered navigation, and this branch carries it.

A specification and a refinement. The main statements of part II are about RawProof.verify, a Lean definition of the reader's check. Step 11 proves that production's verifier, extracted unchanged, refines it: whatever production accepts, the definition accepts. So the soundness statements hold of production's verifier. The definitions of step 9 are a specification that Lean checks against the Rust, not a trusted model; the structures that describe the database behind the root stay assumptions.

Residual risks

What remains after every step passes. Each is a property of the approach or a gap. R1 to R7 belong to part I and R8 to R13 to part II. None is a flaw in a proof, and no independent reviewer has gone through either part.

#RiskLinkWhat would close it
R1The translation from Rust to Lean is wrong in a way that changes what the verifier means.L3An audit of hax, Charon, Aeneas and their library models, or a second extraction route that agrees.
R2The proved call is not the deployed one. Both run production’s reconstruct::reconstruct, but the client fixes one item of a tree with one peak and a trusted size and hashes through symbolic terms, while production hashes bytes. Tests on trees of up to 64 leaves tie the two verdicts.L2State the theorems for any leaf and inactive count, which the reconstruction lemmas already allow, and prove production’s byte terms an instance of the symbolic ones.
