Auditing the Merkle inclusion proof

Eight steps for checking that the Hax → DyLean proof of Merkle inclusion says what the report claims about the Rust that production runs. Wherever one piece of code must agree with another, both are shown side by side with what links them.

The proof lives on branch verify/qmdb-hax-dylean, in the checkout that holds this page. The report states its results; this guide is for the person who checks them. It is written for a reader who knows Rust and Merkle trees and has not used Lean or DyLean. No step asks you to read a proof.

Production baseline: b45ef2d78ea7535d8a51588912ba1e13e461b146. The panels and tables marked as generated are built from the sources by verification/audit/build.py, and check.sh fails when they are stale.

Out of scope: the hax pipeline. This audit assumes that hax, Charon and Aeneas translate the Rust faithfully into Lean, and that their models of the Rust core library match Rust. It does not audit them. Step 4 only checks how the pipeline is used: which tools are pinned, that regenerating the Lean reproduces the committed files, and that nothing was filled in by hand. A translation bug that changes the meaning of the verifier would leave every theorem here true of the wrong program.

Scope and assumptions

In scope: the Merkle target of the Hax → DyLean experiment. That is the production files it shares with the proof, the verification-only client, the handwritten Lean that states what the Rust means, the application assumption, the protocol model, the theorem statements and the checks that a machine runs. The BLS target is out of scope; it shares steps 4 to 7.

Four things are assumed and not audited. For each, the audit still checks how the proof depends on it.

#Assumed, not auditedWhat the audit still checksStep
A1hax, Charon and Aeneas translate the Rust faithfully. Their models of the Rust core library match Rust: integer overflow, a shift that is too large and an index out of bounds are failures.The tool pins and where the binaries come from. Regenerating the Lean is byte-identical. Assumptions/FunsExternal.lean declares nothing.4, 7
A2Lean’s kernel and its three standard axioms (propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound).The axiom audit covers every theorem, and it fails when an escape hatch is planted.7
A3DyLean’s core: traces, what the attacker knows, reachability and its tactics.Its pin, the patch applied to it, the three #combine blocks that define what statements mean, and the two actions accept and receive.6
A4The hash is ideal. A digest is DyLean’s hash constructor applied to its input, as ProVerif and Tamarin treat a hash by default: two digests are equal only when their inputs are the same term, and concatenation keeps the boundary between parts. SHA-256 allows length extension and the ideal hash does not, which is irrelevant here: extension derives the digest of a longer input from a digest alone, so it matters only when part of an input is secret, and every input the verifier hashes is public or chosen by the attacker.What the reading of the hash says, against what production hashes. What the ideal hash cannot express is written down.5

The trust chain

A theorem about generated Lean says something about production only if every link below holds. Each step of the audit discharges named links, and each side-by-side panel carries a label that says how strong its link is today.

LinkWhat must holdStrength todayStep

The labels: proved a named Lean lemma states the link and Lean checks it. tested a test samples it. trusted a person must read both sides; nothing checks it. assumed one of A1 to A4. same code one side calls or includes the other. no link the two sides should agree and nothing relates them.

Order. Start step 7 first and let it run: it builds the toolchain and takes the longest. Then work through steps 0 to 6 in order. Steps 3, 5 and 6 are where a person’s judgement matters most. The times are rough and add up to about a day.

Every Lean declaration, by trust type

The trust chain says which links hold. This graph says which Lean a person has to read. It holds every Lean declaration of the Merkle target: the handwritten definitions and theorems, the generated definitions they reach, and the three libraries under both. Lean prints the graph, and the trust type of each declaration follows from it.

  • cited statement A theorem that the report cites. Its statement is a reading that step 1 checks. Lean checks its proof.
  • trusted Handwritten, and a cited statement depends on what it says. Steps 5 and 6 read these.
  • generated Produced from Rust by hax. Assumed faithful (A1); step 4 looks at four of them.
  • assumed library DyLean (A3), the Aeneas library and hax’s core models (A1).
  • checked A lemma, or a definition that only proofs use. Lean checks it, and it is not to be read.

The rule behind the colours: start from a cited statement and follow what its statement mentions, then what the bodies of those definitions mention, and so on. Never enter a proof. Whatever is reached is what the statement says. A mistake in a checked declaration can make the build fail. It cannot change what a cited statement says.

The target has 197 declarations. 8 are statements that the report cites. What those statements say depends on 37 handwritten declarations, on 39 generated ones and on 3 libraries. The other 110 are lemmas and definitions that only proofs use: Lean checks them, and no cited statement depends on what they say.
